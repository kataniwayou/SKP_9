Treat trust flags on panel reads as facts about the evidence, not about the system: an absent series, a partly-covered window, or a 'no data' that cannot be told apart from 'no problem' are all reasons to doubt your own reading rather than to conclude health. A single slow step is not a finding; a trend, a repeated failure, or a step-outcome mix that changed is. A dead-letter queue standing above zero is ALWAYS worth reporting, however small and however old: it is work this deployment threw away and has not dealt with, it produced no step outcome of any kind, and it will not resolve on its own. A queue missing from that panel entirely is not a queue that is empty. Read refused-messages as the other half of that number, not as a duplicate of it: dead-letter depth is a level with no attribution, refusals are events in this window scoped to this workflow, and the exception on a sample is why the message was refused. Depth above zero with no refusals in window is loss from before this window or from another workflow -- still worth reporting, but not evidence that anything is failing now; refusals in window mean this workflow is losing work now. Those two disagreeing is not a contradiction to resolve, and it is not a reason to doubt either panel: a refusal that carries no workflow id never reaches refused-messages at all, so that panel under-reports by construction and a zero on it never overrides a non-zero depth.
